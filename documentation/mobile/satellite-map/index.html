    Satellite map and flight-path planner¶

    Source

    Snapshot of docs/SATELLITE_MAP.md from TNG-Blue/JetbotNanoOWL at commit d3d7467 (submodule Mobile/Android/JetbotNanoOWL/Qt). Regenerate with Scripts/mkdocs_hooks/snapshot_submodules.py after updating the submodule.

    core/src/geo.cpp (projection, distances, local frame), core/src/flight_plan.cpp (checks, summary, CRC), app/src/map/satellitemap.cpp (tiles and overlay), app/src/map/flightplanmodel.cpp (the QML model Plan), app/qml/pages/MapPage.qml. Host tests: core/tests/test_main.cpp (geo, mission), tests/tst_frontend.cpp (flightPlanEditAndFiles).

    1. Projection¶

    XYZ tile servers (Esri, OpenStreetMap, Google…) all use Web Mercator, EPSG:3857: the sphere of radius a = 6 378 137 m projected conformally (Snyder, Map Projections — A Working Manual, USGS PP 1395, §7; NGA.SIG.0011 for the EPSG definition). In pixels of the world at zoom z (origin top-left, 256 · 2^z px wide):

    x = (λ + 180°) / 360°                      · 256 · 2^z
y = (1 − asinh(tan φ) / π) / 2             · 256 · 2^z       (asinh(tan φ) = ln(tan φ + sec φ))
φ = atan(sinh(π (1 − 2y / (256 · 2^z))))                     (inverse)
|φ| ≤ 85.05112878°  makes the world square
r(φ, z) = 2π a cos φ / (256 · 2^z)  m/px                     (ground resolution)

    Checked against values that follow from the algebra, not from a recorded run: (0°, 0°) at the centre of the z0 tile; (atan(sinh(π/2)), 90°) = (66.513°, 90°) at (¾, ¼) of the world; 85.0511° at the top edge; unproject∘project = id to 1e-9°.

    Tile level against physical pixels. z_t = ⌊z + log₂ dpr⌋ (clamped to the source's range), drawn scaled by 2^(z − z_t) in logical pixels, so the imagery is as sharp as the panel (dpr 3.5 on a Galaxy S21 Ultra). A tile not yet loaded is drawn from its nearest cached ancestor — the 2^−d sub-square of tile (x ≫ d, y ≫ d) at level z_t − d — never left black.

    Caches. 160 decoded tiles in memory (≈ 40 MB, LRU); 256 MB on disk (QNetworkDiskCache), so an area viewed once at home is there offline in the field. Requests: centre-out, at most 6 at a time, a failed tile retried after 30 s.

    Sources. Esri World Imagery (default), OpenStreetMap, or a custom {z}/{x}/{y} template. Each source's attribution is drawn on the map at all times, and requests carry the app's User-Agent, as their terms of use ask. There is deliberately no bulk "download this area": both providers' terms forbid it.

    Scale bar. The ground length 1, 2 or 5 × 10^k m closest to 110 dp at the centre latitude. Mercator's scale grows as sec φ, so the bar is exact at the centre only.

    2. Distances and the local frame¶

    • Legs and lengths: haversine on the sphere of the IUGG mean radius R₁ = 6 371 008.8 m — the same formula and radius as the remote's relay_haversine_m, so the length on the phone is the length the pilot is asked to accept. Against WGS-84 the sphere errs by ≤ 0.5 % (Karney, J. Geodesy 87, 2013); it is a summary for the operator, not a navigation quantity.
    • Insert into the nearest leg: the point-to-segment distance in the local equirectangular plane at the tap (x = R Δλ cos φ, y = R Δφ), exact to second order in leg length / R — ample for choosing among legs of ≤ 2 km.
    • Tracking frame: WGS-84 → ECEF → ENU (Groves, eq. 2.150) and back by Vermeille's closed form (J. Geodesy 76, 2002); round trip exact to 1e-6 m in the tests.

    3. The plan, in the form the aircraft receives¶

    Every waypoint is quantised to the 12-byte rflink_mission item (1e-7°, 0.1 m above home, 0.1 m/s, 1 s). Checks, lengths and the CRC are computed on the quantised values, so what the phone validates is bit for bit what the remote validates (relay_logic.c) and what the flight controller verifies:

    check limit source
    waypoints ≤ 32 RFLINK_MISSION_MAX
    position |φ| ≤ 90°, |λ| ≤ 180°
    altitude above home 0 … 120 m (integer decimetres: 120.04 m passes, 120.06 m does not) EU 2019/947 UAS.OPEN.010
    leg ≤ 2 000 m RELAY_LEG_MAX_M (LoRa range margin)

    The Jetson's planner then runs geofence, keep-out and energy checks on the converted plan; the phone has no map of those and does not pretend to.

    Time estimate: Σ leg_i / v_i + Σ hold_i, with v_i the waypoint's speed or, where it is 0, the planner's cruise speed (max_speed_mps = 2.0 m/s in mission_planner_params.yaml, editable under Plan defaults). No acceleration, climb or wind is modelled, and the page says so.

    End action: return home, land at the last waypoint, or hover there (RFLINK_END_*).

    4. Editing¶

    gesture (edit mode) effect
    tap append a waypoint at the default altitude and speed
    long press insert into the nearest leg, at that leg's altitude
    drag a waypoint move it
    tap a waypoint select it: altitude 0–120 m, speed (0 = planner default), hold 0–60 s
    one-finger drag elsewhere, pinch, wheel pan, zoom about the fingers

    Plans are saved as JSON in <AppData>/plans ({"format": "jetbot-plan", "version": 1, "end_action", "waypoints": [{lat, lon, alt, speed, hold}]}); the current plan survives a restart. A file that is not a plan is refused and leaves the current plan untouched; the round trip keeps the CRC (tst_frontend).

    5. Sending it¶

    The buttons follow the command arbiter (REMOTE_LINK.md §1): Send to remote when the remote is connected (the pilot accepts and starts it on the remote), Upload to Jetson only in DIRECT mode, disabled otherwise with the reason shown. Progress comes from the remote's MISSION message (items the aircraft acknowledged) or from the MAVLink upload (items the Jetson requested).
