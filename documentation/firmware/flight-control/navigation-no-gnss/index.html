    3-D navigation without GPS/GNSS — derivation, verification and limits¶

    Source

    Snapshot of docs/NAVIGATION_NO_GNSS.md from TNG-Blue/JetOwl_FlightControl at commit ffcc11c (submodule Microcontroller/JetOwl_FlightControl). Regenerate with Scripts/mkdocs_hooks/snapshot_submodules.py after updating the submodule.

    Code: Application/Control/{Inc,Src}/nav_dr.{h,c} (pure math, no HAL), Application/Interface/{Inc,Src}/nav_app.{h,c} (FreeRTOS task), topics in uros_app.h. Verification: tests/host/test_nav_dr.c (860 checks). Parameters: airframe_config.h §11. c_D identification: tools/nav_drag_ident.py. Downward-facing LiDAR (DFRobot LPB40B): §13.

    0. Stated up front: what can and cannot be done¶

    Without GNSS (or external references: camera, UWB, scanning lidar) horizontal position always drifts — that is a theorem, not an implementation limitation (§5: p_x, p_y are not in the observable subspace of any combination of IMU + rotor speed + barometer + downward-facing LiDAR — a single-point LiDAR measures distance along the vertical, §13.6). The only thing that can be done is to slow down the drift rate and report the uncertainty correctly so the layer above (the MPC on the Jetson) knows how far to trust it.

    Method Horizontal error after 60 s hover (simulation, §11) Notes
    Pure INS (double integration of the accelerometer) 46 m drifts ~ t² from bias, t³ᐟ² from noise
    Rotor thrust + drag model from encoders (this module) 6.7 m (fixed yaw) — 4.6 m (yaw sweep) drift ~ linear, filter reports σ ≈ 6–7 m (consistent)
    80 m square, yaw along the flight path, indoors 1.6 m final, 2.1 m maximum (2.6 % of distance)
    Outdoors, steady wind 3 m/s + gusts 0.3 m/s 39 m after 64 s without a wind state: 180 m
    Altitude (BMP280 barometer + accelerometer z axis) RMS 2–3 cm observable, does not drift (except for weather, §8)
    + downward-facing LPB40B LiDAR, indoor hover altitude RMS 8 mm (barometer 21 mm), AGL 5 mm §13
    + LiDAR, 80 m square over a 0.4 m box + 5 % slope height above ground (AGL) RMS 2 cm (barometer + flat-floor assumption: 45 cm) §13

    For a position that does not drift, an external position/velocity measurement is required — this filter already has the framework to add one (§12: optical flow from JetOwl_Vision is the most sensible next step).

    1. Why motor speed (encoder) is not aircraft velocity — and where it is used¶

    For a wheeled vehicle, an encoder measures distance because the wheel rolls without slipping on the ground. A multirotor touches nothing: fast-spinning rotors do not mean the aircraft is moving fast. The relationship goes through aerodynamics:

    1. Thrust (momentum theory, Leishman 2006 ch. 2; already used in mixer.c): T = k_f Σ ωᵢ².
    2. Rotor drag (induced drag + blade flapping): each rotor produces a horizontal force opposing the velocity relative to the air, proportional to rotor speed (Martin & Salaün 2010; Mahony, Kumar & Corke 2012, section Blade flapping and induced drag):
    F_d = −K_d (Σ ωᵢ) D Rᵀ (v − w),     D = diag(1, 1, 0)   (body frame)

    Why it is linear in ω·v: flapping angle ∝ v/(ωR), force ∝ T·angle ∝ ω²·v/ω = ω·v.

    The accelerometer measures specific force = (sum of non-gravitational forces)/m. In flight:

    y_a = −(a_T + δ) e₃ − μ D Rᵀ (v − w₃) + Rᵀ a_d₃ + b_a + n_a              (3)
a_T = c_T Σ ωᵢ²,  c_T = k_f/m          μ = c_D Σ ωᵢ,  c_D = K_d/m

    ⇒ the accelerometer's x and y axes directly measure velocity relative to the air, with a coefficient μ that we know at every instant thanks to the rotor speed measured by the encoders (via ESC/DShot telemetry). That is where the encoder enters the problem — not "distance = revolutions × circumference". This is the core idea of Leishman, Macdonald, Beard & McLain 2014 and Abeywardena et al. 2013.

    δ collects the thrust model error (wrong k_f/m, ground effect, translational lift), a_d is the unmodelled aerodynamic disturbance force (§3), w is the horizontal wind.

    2. Why the in-chip AHRS tilt is NOT used¶

    The BNO08x/BNO055 (and Mahony) treat −y_a as the direction of gravity. By (3), on a multirotor −y_a is always close to the body z axis (thrust) plus drag — not gravity when accelerating (Martin & Salaün 2010). In steady state the AHRS sets R̂ᵀe₃ ∥ −(f + b_a), so R̂ y_a + g e₃ loses its low-frequency horizontal component: the AHRS absorbs both the acceleration and the bias into the tilt — erasing exactly the signal we need.

    The first prototype (cascaded form: chip tilt as input, translation estimated separately) showed exactly this phenomenon in simulation: only about half of the horizontal bias was learned and the velocity had a systematic offset of ~0.1 m/s (5–9 m after 60 s hover), even with the bias–tilt coupling modelled in steady state. Therefore the filter estimates attitude itself (gyro integration, corrected via (3)) as in Leishman et al.; from the chip it takes only yaw (magnetometer reference). The flight control loop's attitude is still the chip's — two independent sources.

    A side effect measured in simulation: the chip's yaw is also wrong in proportion to its own tilt error when accelerating (≈ tan(magnetic inclination)·tilt error). Therefore chip yaw only updates at 1 Hz with σ 0.1 rad (NAV_YAW_DIV, NAV_YAW_SIGMA) so that consecutive updates are nearly independent.

    3. State model (ESKF, 21 error states)¶

    NED frame (origin = initialisation point), FRD body, R = R(q) body→NED, n = R e₃, P_n = I − n nᵀ = R D Rᵀ.

    Nominal Error Dynamics
    p (3) NED δp ṗ = v
    v (3) relative to the ground δv see below
    q body→NED δθ (3) local, body frame: q = q̂ ⊗ Exp(δθ) q̇ = ½ q ⊗ (ω_m − b_g)
    b_a (3) δb_a random walk q_ba
    b_g (3) δb_g random walk q_bg
    δ (1) δδ random walk q_δ
    w (2) wind N, E δw random walk q_w
    a_d (2) disturbance force N, E δa_d first-order Gauss–Markov ȧ_d = −a_d/τ + n, stationary variance σ_ad²
    z_g (1) ground (D, NED) at the LiDAR beam hit point δz_g Wiener in hit-point distance travelled (ISO 8608, §13.2); not initialised until LiDAR is available (P row/column zero)

    Two modes, chosen by physical condition:

    • AIDED when armed ∧ RPM of every rotor (12, AIRFRAME_N_ROTORS) valid ∧ a_T n_z ≥ (1 − 3σ_cT) g (model thrust minus 3σ of the identification error is enough to lift the weight ⇒ no ground reaction force left; probability of mistake ≤ 0.13 %): 

      v̇ = g e₃ − (a_T + δ) n − μ P_n (v − w₃) + a_d₃         (the accelerometer is a MEASUREMENT (3))

    • INERTIAL in all other cases (on the ground, RPM lost, fast descent): strapdown INS v̇ = R (y_a − b_a) + g e₃ — always physically correct because the accelerometer measures all forces. A mistaken AIDED→INERTIAL switch in mid-air only temporarily loses the drag feedback.

    Why a_d is a state and not white noise q_v. The real aerodynamic disturbance force (drag model error, vortices near walls/floor) is correlated over ~1 s — the same order as the filter's correction time constant. Treating it as white makes the filter optimistic by 1.6–1.8× (measured by Monte Carlo: error RMS 17 m while the reported σ is 8 m). Adding it to the state using the Singer (1970) model is the standard shaping filter (Bar-Shalom ch. 8; Gelb 1974 §3.8). And a_d must appear in measurement (3): it is a non-gravitational force, so the accelerometer measures it — without the term Rᵀa_d₃ the block NEES (tilt, bias) rises to 2.5–3.5/dimension right after take-off; with it, NEES ≤ 1/dimension throughout the flight (§4.4).

    4. Prediction, discretisation, update¶

    4.1 Prediction (previous IMU sample → this sample)¶

    • Gyro and RPM: average of two samples (trapezoidal, local error O(h³)). Accelerometer (INERTIAL input): previous sample held ZOH — the current sample is a measurement; using it twice would correlate the noise.
    • Attitude: q⁺ = q ⊗ Exp((ω_m − b_g) h) (Solà 2017 eq. 260, exact).
    • AIDED translation with R_m at the step midpoint. P_n is a projection (P_n² = P_n), so the matrix exponential has an exact closed form: 
      exp(−μh P_n) = I − (1 − e⁻ˣ) P_n,     x = μh
G₁ = ∫₀ʰ exp(−μτP_n)dτ = h (I − (1 − φ₁) P_n)
G₂ = ∫₀ʰ∫₀ᵗ exp(..)    = h² (½I − (½ − φ₂) P_n)
φ₁ = (1 − e⁻ˣ)/x,  φ₂ = (x − 1 + e⁻ˣ)/x²      (6-term Taylor series when x < 0.1)
v⁺ = E v + (I − E) w₃ + G₁ f,   p⁺ = p + G₁ v + (hI − G₁) w₃ + G₂ f,   f = g e₃ − (a_T+δ) n + a_d₃
      (exact ZOH, Franklin–Powell–Workman §4.3). test_translation_zoh: matches a 13×13 expm to 1.95·10⁻⁷ (float precision) over 5 cases including x = 1.
    • Disturbance force: a_d⁺ = e^(−h/τ) a_d (exact).

    4.2 Error matrices¶

    δṗ = δv
AIDED:    δv̇ = −μ P_n (δv − δw₃) − n δδ + δa_d₃ + [A_T R [e₃]× − μ R (D[s_B]× − [D s_B]×)] δθ
INERTIAL: δv̇ = −R [y_a − b_a]× δθ − R δb_a                          (Solà 2017 eq. 237)
δθ̇ = −[ω̂]× δθ − δb_g ;   δȧ_d = −δa_d/τ

    (s = v − w₃, s_B = Rᵀs, A_T = a_T + δ). Φ = I + Fh + (Fh)²/2 (truncation error (|F|h)³/6 ≈ 2·10⁻⁵ at 200 Hz). test_jacobians: Φ matches a central finite difference of the nominal map, rewritten independently in double (RK4 with 200 substeps), to 1.2·10⁻⁵ (AIDED) and 2.9·10⁻⁷ (INERTIAL).

    Process noise: residual white q_v per CWNA (Bar-Shalom §6.2.2), gyro DWNA h²σ_g², INERTIAL adds accelerometer input noise DWNA Σ = R diag(r) Rᵀ (§6.3.2), random walk q·h, exact Gauss–Markov σ_ad² (1 − e^(−2h/τ)).

    4.3 Update¶

    Each scalar measurement sequentially (valid for diagonal R, Simon 2006 §3.4), Joseph form computed explicitly (Simon §5.4), gate χ²₀.₉₉₉(1) = 10.83; after each measurement the error is injected into the nominal state and P ← G P Gᵀ is reset, G_θθ = I − [δθ/2]× (Solà §7.2).

    Measurement When Jacobian (non-zero)
    Accelerometer (3) AIDED H_v = −μDRᵀ, H_w = μDRᵀ[:,0:2], H_θ = −μD[s_B]× + [Rᵀa_d₃]×, H_ba = I, H_δ = −e₃, H_ad = Rᵀ[:,0:2]
    Chip yaw 1 Hz H_θ = [0, R₂₁R₁₃ − R₁₁R₂₃, R₁₁R₂₂ − R₂₁R₁₂]/(R₁₁² + R₂₁²)
    Barometer every new sample (25 Hz) H_pz = −1 (§8)
    LiDAR r = (z_g − p_z − e₃ᵀRl)/c, c = e₃ᵀRu every /range sample (100 Hz), delay τ H_pz = −1/c, H_vz = τ/c, H_zg = 1/c, H_θ = (1/c)(g_b × (l + r u))ᵀA, H_bg = τH_θ (§13)

    test_jacobians: accelerometer + yaw H match finite differences to 3.4·10⁻⁸.

    5. What is observable — and the operational consequences¶

    test_observability: 20 s discrete observability Gramian, hover, checking both the rank and each derived direction:

    Yaw Rank Unobservable directions
    fixed 14/20 p_x, p_y; (v, w) offset together on each axis; (δθ_⊥, δb_a, δ(v − w)) with g δθ = μ δ(v−w) = δb_a
    rotating steadily 0.3 rad/s 14/20 as above, "rotating with the body" (horizontal gyro bias keeps the tilt fixed in the body)
    varying (1.0 sin 0.3t) 16/20 p_x, p_y; (v, w) offset together
    fixed / varying, + LiDAR 15/21 / 17/21 as above; z_g becomes observable (§13.6). Without LiDAR: z_g (not initialised) is the 21st unobservable direction

    5.1 Meaning¶

    • Horizontal position is never observable → it drifts, P_pp grows and reports exactly that.
    • Wind and ground velocity are only visible as the difference v − w (drag sees velocity relative to the air). Ground velocity comes from integrating the dynamics with the initial condition v = 0 (ZUPT) ⇒ the wind is learned right after leaving the ground (simulation: ŵ ≈ (1.5, −2.5) m/s after 1 s, (2.0, −2.4) after 5 s; truth (2.0, −2.24) plus gusts σ 0.3 m/s); after that the error grows with the variability of the wind (q_w), not with its magnitude.
    • The tilt–bias pair cannot be separated at fixed yaw, but the unobservable direction on the ground (μ = 0, v = 0) is different from the one in flight ⇒ ZUPT + gravity before take-off cut it within the first few seconds; changing yaw after take-off makes it observable (rank 16). Simulation over 8 pairs: a ±1 rad yaw sweep reduces the 60 s error from 6.7 m to 4.6 m.

    5.2 Recommended flight procedure¶

    1. Keep the aircraft stationary on the ground for ≥ 2 s after power-on (ZUPT + gravity; nav_mode = 1, zupt = 1).
    2. Arm, take off vertically.
    3. Right after reaching altitude: sweep yaw ±60…90° twice (do not rotate steadily in one direction — useless, §5).
    4. Land, disarm: ZUPT locks the velocity again (but not the position — §0).

    5.3 Scale error from c_D¶

    In steady state μ v = force ⇒ an x % error in c_D makes the air-relative velocity wrong by ~x %, i.e. an along-track error of ~x % of the distance. Simulation with c_D off by +20 %: maximum error 4.4 m on the 80 m square (vs. 2.1 m when correct). ⇒ c_D must be identified (§6).

    6. Identifying c_D without GNSS¶

    From (3) with yaw held constant over a flight segment (take-off → landing), body x axis:

    d = ∫ v_Bx dt = −a i + β j,      a = 1/c_D,  β = b_x/c_D
i = ∫ y_x / Σω dt  (nav_id_ix),   j = ∫ dt / Σω  (nav_id_j)

    d is the distance measured with a tape between two marks, projected onto the body x axis (cos(tilt) error ≈ 0.5 % at 0.1 rad) — no estimated yaw, no magnetometer, no GNSS needed. The firmware accumulates i, j during AIDED steps and closes the segment at the next ZUPT (/jetowl/dr/status elements 11–15; nav_id_seq increments when a new segment is available).

    Procedure (indoors, no wind):

    1. Mark A and B 10–20 m apart (tape). Place the aircraft at A, nose pointing at B.
    2. Take off, fly forward to B, holding yaw, land at B, disarm. Record (d = +AB, i, j).
    3. Take off at B, keep the same yaw, fly backward to A, land. Record (d = −AB, i, j).
    4. python3 tools/nav_drag_ident.py AB i₁ j₁ −AB i₂ j₂ → NAV_C_D. Add more forward/backward pairs for least squares; the tool refuses when the condition number > 50 (missing opposite-direction segment).

    test_drag_identification: 2 flights of 15 m forward/backward in the full simulation → c_D error 0.69 %, b_x = 0.037 (truth 0.040). Outdoors with wind, d is the distance relative to the ground while i measures relative to the air ⇒ identify only in calm air.

    7. Stationary detector (ZUPT)¶

    Windowed variance (the MV detector of Skog et al. 2010) for both the accelerometer and the gyro:

    T = (1/W)[ Σ‖y_k − ȳ‖²/σ_a² + Σ‖ω_k − ω̄‖²/σ_g² ]
Under H₀ (stationary, white Gaussian noise): W·T ~ χ²(6W − 6)   (sample variance loses 3 degrees of freedom/sensor)
γ = χ²₁₋α(6W − 6)/W     (Wilson–Hilferty 1931; normal quantile A&S 26.2.23, |error| < 4.5·10⁻⁴)
plus   | ‖ȳ‖ − g | ≤ 3√(σ_b0² + σ_a²/W)   and  ‖ω̄‖ ≤ 3√(3(σ_bg0² + σ_g²/W))

    ZUPT is applied only when the rotors are stopped (disarmed or all 4 ESCs report stopped — a quad cannot fly with its rotors stopped) and H₀ is accepted. The "gyro energy" Σ‖ω‖² (ARE detector) is not used: the gyro bias remaining after the chip's calibration (0.002 rad/s) is larger than the per-sample noise (0.0014 rad/s), so ARE rejects H₀ even when stationary — this was encountered in the first test run. test_zupt_detector: stationary accepted 98.7 % (expected 1 − α = 99 %; the windows overlap, so the standard deviation of the ratio is ~0.6 %); hand-carried walking 0.3 m/s² 1 Hz: 0.94 %; slow rotation 0.05 rad/s: 0 %; free fall: 0 %. After 60 s stationary, tilt converges to truth ± |b|/g (0.0061 rad, |b|/g = 0.0051) even though the chip is off by 3°.

    8. Barometer¶

    ICAO standard atmosphere (Doc 7488/3), troposphere: h = (T_ref/L)(1 − (p/p₀)^(R*L/(g₀M))), R*L/(g₀M) = 0.190263, T₀/L = 44330.77 m (also the constant in Bosch BST-BMP280-DS001 §3.6). R_h = (dh/dp)² σ_p²: at sea level |dh/dp| = 0.0832 m/Pa ⇒ σ_h = 0.10 m with 1.2 Pa (table 8). p₀ = first sample after initialisation (origin offset ≤ 1σ_h, constant). Set NAV_BARO_T_REF_K to the air temperature measured on site: a 10 K offset ⇒ 3.5 % altitude scale error. Atmospheric pressure changes with the weather (~0.1 hPa/10 min ≈ 0.8 m) — not compensated yet; long flights must land back at the origin to re-anchor.

/range (Jetson, 100 Hz) ──► uros on_range ──► NavApp_PushRange (queue of 4) ┤
                                                                           ▼
nav task (AboveNormal1, poll 2 ms, each new IMU sample exactly once, dt from SH-2 timestamps)
   nav_dr_step / nav_dr_update_baro / nav_dr_update_range ──► NavApp_GetState ──► uros (50 Hz / 2 Hz)
    • Position does not enter a control loop on the FC (the position loop/MPC runs on the Jetson), so the ESKF runs in its own task (≈3·10⁴ MAC/sample, ~1 % of the Cortex-M55), not in the 1 kHz interrupt.
    • w_valid only when all 4 RPM frames are valid and their age ≤ NAV_RPM_MAX_AGE_S; otherwise ⇒ INERTIAL (open loop with a reason: no rotor speed sensor is left; the INS remains physically correct, P grows correctly).
    • Topics (uros_app.h), frame odom = ENU, origin = initialisation point:
    Topic Type Contents CDR size
    /jetowl/dr/pose geometry_msgs/PoseWithCovarianceStamped ESKF position + attitude, 6×6 cov 368 B
    /jetowl/dr/twist geometry_msgs/TwistWithCovarianceStamped velocity relative to the ground (ENU, frame odom) + angular rate 360 B
    /jetowl/dr/status std_msgs/Float32MultiArray (20) mode, zupt, σ_h, σ_z, NIS, wind, δ, nav_id_*, AGL, σ_AGL, LiDAR NIS, number of z_g initialisations 92 B
    /range (in) sensor_msgs/Range downward-facing LPB40B LiDAR from lpb40b_node (§13) ~60 B

    All < 512 B MTU (best effort does not fragment).     12. Limits and upgrade paths¶

    • Position drift is unavoidable (§0, §5). Stopping the drift requires an external measurement; the ESKF framework easily accepts additional scalar measurements:
      • Optical flow (JetOwl_Vision camera) + the distance to the ground already available (LPB40B LiDAR, §13, giving the optical flow scale): measures velocity relative to the ground ⇒ (v, w) become separable, drift drops by an order. The most sensible next step.
      • Visual landmarks/AprilTag or UWB: measure position ⇒ no drift.
    • A raw magnetometer instead of the chip's yaw would remove the yaw error caused by the chip's tilt error (§2).
    • Airframe drag (∝ v²) is not yet modelled — at high speed (> 5 m/s) it ends up in a_d and the effective c_D; identify c_D at realistic flight speeds.
    • Barometer: weather drift and prop-wash effects near the ground are not yet compensated.
    • All results are simulation + mathematical verification; there are no real flight logs yet. Verification order per control-systems §4: simulation (done) → SIL (Gazebo hexacopter_sim) → HIL → bench → flight.

    13. Downward-facing LiDAR (DFRobot LPB40B) — distance to the ground¶

    The sensor already has a driver on the Jetson side (Isaac_ROS-Jetbot_NanoOWL/Source/ROS2_Lidar_SLAM, lpb40b_node), so the FC does not read its UART but receives it over micro-ROS:

    LPB40B ─UART 115200─► Jetson lpb40b_node (CRC-8 0x31, status code → REP-117, Hampel 5 samples, 3σ)
   ─► /range  sensor_msgs/Range, stamp = MEASUREMENT time (100 Hz) ─► micro-ROS agent ─UDP─► FC uros_app
   ─► NavApp_PushRange (stamp → FC clock: t_fc = (stamp − t₀)/1000, t₀ from rmw_uros_sync_session)
   ─► nav task: right after the first IMU step with t ≥ t_meas ─► nav_dr_update_range(r, τ = t_IMU − t_meas)

    Sensor → filter → estimator chain: Hampel outlier filter (window 5, 3σ) in lpb40b_node; χ² 0.999 gate in the ESKF; the ESKF estimates z_g together with p, v, q. There is no new controller: AGL and p_z go out on /jetowl/dr/* to the MPC on the Jetson (closed altitude loop).

    13.1 Measurement model¶

    Sensor at l, unit beam u (FRD body frame). Locally flat horizontal ground at coordinate D = z_g. The ray p + Rl + sRu hits the ground when e₃ᵀ(p + Rl + sRu) = z_g:

    r = n / c,   n = z_g − p_z − e₃ᵀRl,   c = e₃ᵀRu     (c = cos of the angle between the ray and the vertical)
R = R̂(I + [δθ]×), aᵀ[b]× = (a × b)ᵀ, g_b = Rᵀe₃:
  ∂n/∂δθ = (g_b × l)ᵀ,  ∂c/∂δθ = −(g_b × u)ᵀ
  ⇒ ∂r/∂p_z = −1/c,  ∂r/∂z_g = 1/c,  ∂r/∂δθ = (1/c)(g_b × (l + r u))ᵀ

    Used only when c ≥ cos(ATT_CMD_TILT_MAX) (35°): outside the normal flight envelope the measurement is dropped — information is lost, but nothing is made wrong. test_jacobians: H matches finite differences (double) to 7.5·10⁻⁸.

    13.2 Terrain model — from ISO 8608 to q_s¶

    ISO 8608 gives the height spectral density versus spatial frequency n [cycles/m]: G_d(n) = G_d(n₀)(n/n₀)⁻², n₀ = 0.1. A spectrum ∝ n⁻² is the spectrum of a Wiener process in distance travelled. Structure function:

    D(d) = E[(z(s+d) − z(s))²] = 2∫₀^∞ G_d(n)(1 − cos 2πnd) dn = 2π² n₀² G_d(n₀) |d|
       (using ∫₀^∞ (1 − cos an)/n² dn = πa/2)       ⇒  q_s = 2π² n₀² G_d(n₀)  [m²/m]
    ISO 8608 class A B C D E F G H
    G_d(n₀) [10⁻⁶ m³] 16 64 256 1024 4096 16384 65536 262144
    q_s [m²/m] 3.2e-6 1.26e-5 5.1e-5 2.0e-4 8.1e-4 3.23e-3 1.3e-2 5.2e-2

    A 2-D surface whose structure function is linear in distance (Lévy Brownian field) gives exactly D(d) along every straight line. z_g is the ground at the hit point of the most recently processed measurement. For each new measurement: Q_gg = q_s E|Δhit|, with hit(t_m) = p_h(t_m) + (R_m(l + r u))_NE. The previous hit point is recomputed from the same current estimate (propagated back with the gyro) so that the difference contains only the real motion, without the jitter of the attitude updates (r·δθ ~ 4 cm at r = 10 m — the first implementation lacked this and the LiDAR NIS was 5 % low). Jensen for the velocity error: E|Δhit| ≤ √(|Δĥ|² + (P_vn + P_ve)Δt²). When stationary, Q = 0.

    Choosing the class by the steepest slope s that must be tracked. A steady-state random-walk filter (K ≈ √(qΔ/R)) tracks a slope with offset b = sΔ/K = s√(RΔ/q), while σ_zg = (qΔR)^¼. To get b ≤ σ_zg:

    q_s ≥ (s⁴ R Δ)^⅓,   Δ = v_max / f_LiDAR
outdoors s = 10 %, v = 5 m/s, 100 Hz, R = 8.3·10⁻⁴ m²  ⇒  q_s ≥ 1.6·10⁻³  ⇒  class F (default)

    Checked by simulation (80 m square over a 0.4 m box + 5 % slope + 2 cm roughness; barometer only: p_z RMS 0.020 m):

    Class p_z RMS AGL RMS / reported σ_AGL
    D 0.041 m — worse than barometer only: the filter pushes the slope into p_z 0.019 / 0.008 m (2.4× optimistic)
    E 0.022 m 0.019 / 0.011 m (1.7×)
    F (default) 0.019 m 0.020 / 0.017 m (1.2×)
    G 0.020 m 0.021 / 0.029 m (pessimistic)

    Flat indoor floor: class B. Then the LiDAR holds altitude to 8 mm RMS in hover. With class F, during hover the horizontal velocity uncertainty itself (σ_v ~ 0.1 m/s ⇒ possibly drifting onto new terrain) makes P_gg grow ~3 cm/√s — physically correct, but the LiDAR then mainly provides AGL and cannot pin p_z.

    13.3 Delayed measurement¶

    τ ≈ 20 ms (Hampel group delay) + USB + DDS + UDP ⇒ 22–35 ms. Climbing/descending at 2 m/s ⇒ 4–7 cm, more than the sensor σ, so it must be compensated. The nominal state is rolled back to t_m using the stored prediction increments themselves (NAV_DR_HIST = 32 steps, 160 ms at 200 Hz; the oldest step is taken partially, error ≤ |a|h²/8):

    p_z(t_m) = p_z − Σ Δp_z,j,     R_m = R Π_j Exp(−ω̂_j h_j)
δp_z(t_m) = δp_z − τ δv_z,     δθ(t_m) = A(δθ + τ δb_g),   A = R_mᵀR   (R Exp(φ) RᵀR_m = R_m Exp(Aφ))
⇒ H_vz = τ/c,  H_θ = h_θ A,  H_bg = τ h_θ A

    Process noise during the delay interval is correlated with the current state; the Bar-Shalom (2002) approximation adds it to R: q_v τ³/(3c²) + |h_θ|² σ_g² τ h. The second-order term (gτ²/2)δθ < 1 mm is neglected.

    13.4 Measurement noise¶

    R = σ_r² + σ_div² + (ṙ σ_t)² + (the terms of 13.3):

    • σ_r = 0.05/√3 = 0.0289 m: the ±5 cm error (DFRobot SEN0586) treated as uniformly distributed. The 1.5 cm quantisation adds 4.3 mm, less than 3 % of the variance.
    • 2.2° receive angle: a ray tilted φ from the ground normal ⇒ the return point lies within ±r tan φ·FOV/2 ⇒ σ_div² = (r tan φ·FOV/2)²/3.
    • Clock synchronisation ±1 ms (1 kHz tick of microros_time.c): σ_t = 10⁻³/√3, ṙ = −v_z/c + h_θω̂.

    13.5 Initialisation and terrain steps¶

    z_g = p_z(t_m) + e₃ᵀR_m l + c·r ⇒ δz_g = Jδx + c·v_r, J = −c·H (except the z_g entry). Linear transformation of a Gaussian (state augmentation, Solà 2014): P_gx = JP, P_gg = JPJᵀ + c²R. The marginal distribution of every other state is unchanged, so re-initialisation never corrupts p, v, q: test_range_faults checks that p, q, P_pp are bit-for-bit unchanged across a re-initialisation.

    Initialise when (a) the first measurement arrives, or (b) NAV_DR_RANGE_REINIT_N = 3 consecutive measurements are rejected by the χ² gate. Under H₀, the innovations of an optimal filter are white, so P(3 consecutive > 10.83) = (10⁻³)³ = 10⁻⁹ per sample. Therefore 3 consecutive rejections indicate a terrain step (table edge, stair, moving object below), not noise. A single isolated outlier is only rejected and does not cause a re-initialisation.

    13.6 Observability¶

    The LiDAR measures z_g − p_z, the barometer measures p_z ⇒ both are observable (rank +1: 15/21 with fixed yaw, 17/21 with varying yaw). Without the barometer only AGL is observable. The LiDAR does not help p_x, p_y.

Frankly: over 6 seeds × 3 time points, the mean NEES is 21.6 (3 % above expected), 17/18 within the 99 % bounds. With the delay disabled the results are similar, so the source is not the delay compensation; with flat terrain (q_s → 0) it is slightly higher (~7 %), when p_z, z_g are very small. Plus one spurious re-initialisation at r ≈ 39 m in about 2·10⁵ samples. The filter is therefore slightly optimistic when the LiDAR tightly pins the vertical channel, at the level of a few percent.

    13.8 Mounting — the FC and ROS must share the same geometry¶

    airframe_config.h 11.5 (FRD) must match the static TF base_link → lpb40b_link that lidar_dual_launch.py publishes (lpb40b_{x,y,z,roll,pitch,yaw}, FLU, the ray is the +x axis of lpb40b_link): l_FRD = (x, −y, −z); mounted straight down ⇔ pitch = +π/2 ⇔ u_FRD = (0, 0, 1). The 2-D SLAM on the Jetson automatically ignores rays not in the scan plane (elevation gate), so 2-D SLAM can run at the same time. Whatever error NAV_RANGE_LEVER_Z_M has, the AGL is offset by the same amount.

    13.9 Limits¶

    • Low reflectivity (wet grass, dark soil ~10 %) ⇒ range drops to 12 m (SEN0586). Water, glass ⇒ lpb40b_node publishes NaN/+inf and the FC ignores it.
    • Tree canopy, tall grass: the "ground" is the first surface within the receive cone. AGL is the distance to that surface.
    • Real terrain may not match the chosen ISO class. Class too low ⇒ slopes drag p_z (§13.2); class too high ⇒ the LiDAR pins p_z less. Check the LiDAR NIS (/jetowl/dr/status[18]) on flight logs.
    • Not yet run on hardware; the ±1 ms clock synchronisation is an assumption from microros_time.c.
